The problem

Millions of small generators run every day — and vent straight into the open air.

Where the grid is unreliable, petrol and diesel generators fill the gap in homes, shops, and workshops. Each one is a small, uncontrolled smokestack releasing carbon dioxide alongside carbon monoxide, fine particulates, formaldehyde, and volatile organic compounds at street level.

This project asks a practical question: can a compact, low-cost add-on capture a meaningful share of those emissions at the point they leave the exhaust — before they reach the people standing next to the machine?

Aim

Capture and sequester carbon and associated pollutants from generator exhaust using a sequential treatment system.

The work pairs a working bench prototype with field measurements, so every design decision is checked against real readings rather than theory alone.

The system

Two paths to the same goal

The approved design treats the exhaust in sequence. A second approach is under study, exploring a single absorption stage with a chosen sink material.
